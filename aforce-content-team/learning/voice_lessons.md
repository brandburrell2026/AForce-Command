# VOICE LESSONS

Learned rules about how AForce sounds. Written only by the Voice Training Engine (`../workflows/voice-training-engine.md`) from cited evidence. Every lesson: source observation, rule, evidence, confidence, example count, dates. Categories per the five-category system (`../CLAUDE.md`).

### V-001 — The two-beat reversal [BRAND FACT]
**SOURCE OBSERVATION:** Canonical copy repeatedly builds meaning by negating the expected frame, then asserting the AForce frame: "We don't chase performance. We prepare for it." / "Everyone performs. Not everyone prepares." / "Hydration is not a claim. It is a process." / "Not a tagline. A behavioral operating system."
**RULE:** When making a brand point, prefer a short negation beat followed by a shorter assertion beat.
**EVIDENCE:** site-copy-extracts.md (manifesto, homepage, science). **CONFIDENCE:** HIGH · **EXAMPLES:** 7+ · **LEARNED:** 2026-08-19

### V-002 — Short declaratives; fragments on purpose [BRAND FACT]
**SOURCE OBSERVATION:** Brand-surface sentences average well under 12 words; deliberate fragments carry rhythm ("Two brothers. Two moments of stillness. One belief.").
**RULE:** Favor short declarative language over long explanatory introductions. Fragments are allowed when rhythmic, never from laziness.
**EVIDENCE:** all site pages. **CONFIDENCE:** HIGH · **EXAMPLES:** 20+ · **LEARNED:** 2026-08-19

### V-003 — Zero exclamation marks [BRAND FACT]
**SOURCE OBSERVATION:** No exclamation point appears anywhere on the canonical marketing surfaces.
**RULE:** Confidence carries the sentence; the punctuation never does. No exclamation marks in brand-voice copy (creator-voice UGC may differ).
**EVIDENCE:** full-site scan 2026-08-19. **CONFIDENCE:** HIGH · **EXAMPLES:** site-wide · **LEARNED:** 2026-08-19

### V-004 — Precise numbers as premium cues [BRAND FACT]
**SOURCE OBSERVATION:** The brand uses exact figures where competitors use hype: 04:58 AM, pH 8.8, 250 members, 20 servings, 11 fl oz.
**RULE:** Replace vague intensity ("super early", "insanely hydrating") with a specific number or drop the modifier.
**EVIDENCE:** our-story, science, shop. **CONFIDENCE:** HIGH · **EXAMPLES:** 6+ · **LEARNED:** 2026-08-19

### V-005 — "The ___" naming convention [BRAND FACT]
**SOURCE OBSERVATION:** Concepts are named with the definite article: The Ritual, The Standard, The Signal, The Demand, The Moment Before, The Founding 250.
**RULE:** When a concept deserves a name, name it "The ___" — never cute portmanteaus or hashtag-babble.
**EVIDENCE:** site navigation + section titles. **CONFIDENCE:** HIGH · **EXAMPLES:** 8+ · **LEARNED:** 2026-08-19

### V-006 — Claims stay in lanes; honesty is the register [BRAND FACT]
**SOURCE OBSERVATION:** Science copy pairs "what it does (established role)" with "why AFORCE includes it," carries guardrail sentences, and voluntarily narrows claims ("a formulation standard, not a blood-pH claim"; "not a performance promise").
**RULE:** Under-claim and over-explain. Every physiological statement names its lane; transparency is written with pride ("Stated Plainly"), never buried.
**EVIDENCE:** science + shop pages. **CONFIDENCE:** HIGH · **EXAMPLES:** 10+ · **LEARNED:** 2026-08-19

### V-007 — Quiet close; never a beg [BRAND FACT]
**SOURCE OBSERVATION:** Sections end on standards, not pleas: "When the moment begins, the decision has already been made." / "The moment will come. Be ready for it."
**RULE:** End on a standard or an invitation — never "don't forget to like and follow."
**EVIDENCE:** manifesto, homepage closers. **CONFIDENCE:** HIGH · **EXAMPLES:** 5+ · **LEARNED:** 2026-08-19

### V-008 — Verbs carry power; adjectives are rationed [BRAND FACT]
**SOURCE OBSERVATION:** Canonical copy leans on prepare, build, hold, carry, enter, reveal, lock in; superlatives are nearly absent.
**RULE:** Prefer a stronger verb over a stacked adjective. One adjective per sentence is usually the ceiling.
**EVIDENCE:** site-wide. **CONFIDENCE:** MEDIUM-HIGH · **EXAMPLES:** pervasive · **LEARNED:** 2026-08-19

# AFORCE CLAIMS — WHAT CONTENT MAY AND MAY NOT SAY

**Version:** 1.0 · 2026-08-19
**Governed by:** `docs/COMPLIANCE_FRAMEWORK.md` (repo root) and, for anything touching AForce OS intelligence, `governance/CLAIMS-REGISTER.md`. This file translates those rules for marketing content. It is not legal advice; counsel finalizes all public claim language before launch. When this file and counsel disagree, counsel governs.

**The operating rule, from canonical copy:** *Nothing added without a reason. Nothing claimed without a lane.*

---

## 1. The three evidence lanes — never mix them

Every physiological or performance statement in content is one of:

| Lane | Definition | How it must read |
|---|---|---|
| **ESTABLISHED** | Recognized physiology / authoritative sources (e.g., NIH ODS nutrient roles) | Stated plainly, in structure/function form: "contributes to," "supports normal" |
| **EMERGING** | Early or limited research | Explicitly framed as emerging: "early research suggests… evidence is still limited." Requires QA (Agent 18) sign-off before publishing |
| **BRAND INTERPRETATION** | AForce's philosophy and framing | Clearly ours: ritual, preparation, standard — behavioral and cultural, never physiological proof |

If a claim can't be placed in a lane with a source, it does not exist. Flag it; never publish it.

## 2. Approved claim bank — ESTABLISHED lane [usable as-is]

From the canonical science page (NIH ODS-sourced) and standard structure/function form:

- "Water and electrolytes contribute to normal fluid regulation."
- "Sodium contributes to normal fluid balance and supports normal muscle and nerve function."
- "Potassium's cellular gradient is required for normal nerve transmission and muscle contraction."
- "Magnesium participates in energy production and normal nerve and muscle function."
- "Calcium supports normal muscle movement and communication between nerves and muscle."
- "Fluid balance, muscle function, nerve signaling and cognitive function depend on normal physiology."
- "Electrolytes help replace what you lose in sweat." *(general education framing)*
- "Hydration is a process, not a promise" — the chain: ingestion → fluid availability → electrolyte balance → physiological regulation → normal function.
- pH 8.8 (cans): **only** as "a formulation standard, not a blood-pH claim." Never imply body-pH change.

Educational content may teach general hydration physiology (sweat loss, thirst lag, why electrolytes matter) — sourced, framed as general nutrition science, never as AForce-specific clinical outcomes. Canonical guardrail sentence: *"These describe established physiological roles in general nutrition — not AForce-specific clinical outcomes."*

## 3. Product statements confirmed on canonical surfaces

"Plant-based" · "0g added sugar" · "marine minerals" · "alkaline pH" (line-level) · "20 servings per box" · "drop one into any water" · "TSA-simple." Stick-level formulation specifics (pH value, botanicals, amounts): **PENDING — banned in content until `PRODUCTS.md` confirms.**

## 4. Banned — absolute, no exceptions

- **Disease framing:** diagnose, treat, cure, prevent, "helps with [condition]," disease names attached to the product (cramps-as-medical, dehydration-as-illness, kidney anything).
- **Medical vocabulary from the app register (extends to marketing):** deficiency, disorder, injury-prevention, risk reduction as outcome.
- **Overreach:** "boosts immunity," "detoxifies," "cures hangovers," "clinically proven" (nothing is), "doctor recommended" (no such program), "3× the hydration of water" (no substantiation), any % or multiplier without a study we own and counsel approves.
- **Body-pH claims:** anything implying the product alkalizes blood or body.
- **Implied medical endorsement:** surgeons/nurses/first responders appear as *performers with rituals*, never as clinical endorsers of physiological effects.
- **Fabricated evidence:** invented studies, invented statistics, invented testimonials, "studies show" without a named source.

## 5. Comparison and superiority rules

- Never name competitors in claims ("better than X"). Cultural contrast is allowed ("Most hydration brands end at the drink. AForce begins there." — canonical); performance-superiority claims are not.
- "Clean" language: "clean performance" as brand vocabulary is fine; "clean label"-style implications about competitors are not.

## 6. Mandatory disclaimer

Wherever content makes structure/function statements in owned long-form surfaces (site, email, YouTube descriptions), the standard disclaimer applies [FACT — site footer]:

> "Statements regarding hydration, readiness, and performance support have not been evaluated by the Food and Drug Administration. This product is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease. Individual results vary."

Short-form social posts avoid the need by staying in education/culture framing; when a post leans on §2 claims, QA decides whether the disclaimer or a science-page link is required.

## 7. Escalation

1. Writer wants a claim not in §2/§3 → route to Agent 07 (Education + Science) to classify the lane with a source.
2. Agent 18 (QA) verifies lane + phrasing. **QA never silently approves questionable claims — it flags them.**
3. New ESTABLISHED claims are added here by leadership decision; EMERGING claims additionally carry a review date.
4. Anything counsel-sensitive (health language on packaging/ads, testimonials with physiological statements, athlete endorsement claims) → leadership + counsel before use.

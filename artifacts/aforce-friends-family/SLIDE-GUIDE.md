# AForce — Friends & Family Deck Slide Guide

A slide-by-slide explanation of the AForce Friends & Family deck (25 slides). Use this as
speaker notes or a narrative map. The deck is built as one continuous argument:
**a product becomes a ritual, a ritual becomes behavior, behavior becomes
retention, retention becomes a recurring membership business.**

The deck moves through six acts:

| Section | Slides | Job |
|---|---|---|
| **The Stakes** | 1–2 | Set the emotional premise and define who this is for. |
| **The Team** | 3–5 | Establish credibility — the platform, the founders, the advisors. |
| **The Opportunity** | 6–8 | Size the market, expose the gap, claim the white space. |
| **The System** | 9–13 | Show the product is a *system*, not a beverage. |
| **The Plan** | 14–24 | Prove how the system compounds into a defensible, fundable business — and what it returns. |
| **The Close** | 25 | Invite the partnership. |

---

## The Stakes

### Slide 1 — Performance Is Non-Negotiable
The cinematic opener. One image, one truth: for the people AForce serves,
performance isn't optional. It sets the tone — premium, serious, editorial — and
frames everything that follows as high-stakes rather than lifestyle.

### Slide 2 — Built For People Who Don't Get To Be Off
Defines the audience: founders, athletes, operators, creators — people whose
performance matters *every single day*. This narrows the market from "everyone who
drinks beverages" to a specific, high-value, always-on customer.

---

## The Team

### Slide 3 — Selected For America's Real Deal
The credibility hook. AForce was chosen from hundreds of companies for a
nationally televised investment platform airing January 2027. This raise builds the
proof the company walks onto that national stage with — it gives the round urgency
and a concrete deadline.

### Slide 4 — The Founders
Lived conviction. The founders built the standard they needed themselves — this is
framed as a real operating company, not a beverage looking for a story. Establishes
founder–market fit.

### Slide 5 — The Advisors
"The people behind the people." Signals that AForce is backed by operators who have
built, scaled, and exited at the highest level — Peter Ingwersen (cultural
architect / brand builder), Kristel van Kleef (brand strategy & ecosystem design),
Mark Mendel (investment banking, Finalis), and Thomas Masterbouni (CIO, Big Idea
Ventures). De-risks execution.

---

## The Opportunity

### Slide 6 — The Prize
Sizes the market. Performance hydration is evolving beyond "energy" into
readiness, recovery, and daily ritual. Shows an illustrative market size to frame
the upside.

### Slide 7 — The Category Is Noise
The problem with the incumbents. Monster, Celsius, Prime, Ghost, Gatorade — every
brand fights for *attention*. Almost none compete for *composure*. This sets up the
wedge AForce drives in.

### Slide 8 — The White Space
The positioning claim. AForce owns the moment **before execution** — the pause
before performance — the white space the category left empty. The full product
lineup is shown floating in that space.

---

## The System

### Slide 9 — The Ritual
The core behavioral idea in four beats: **Pause. Hydrate. Lock In. Perform.** No
explanation needed — it's a ritual, not a feature list. This is the heartbeat of
the brand.

### Slide 10 — The Three Signals That Matter
The proof metrics that show this is a system, not a drink: **NPS 60+** (people
believe), **60-day retention 60%+** (people stay), **membership conversion 20%+**
(people commit). Together they prove durable behavior.

### Slide 11 — The System
How the business actually works: **products create acquisition; the OS powers and
measures retention.** Hydration is the entry point that leads all the way to
community.

### Slide 12 — The Framework
Six steps, one compounding system: product → ritual → behavior → retention →
membership → scale. Each stage is measurable, each has a KPI, and each earns the
next. Reinforces that the OS is what powers and measures retention.

### Slide 13 — Customer Journey
The full lifecycle from first sip to membership: Discover → Purchase → Activate →
Ritual → Behavior → Results → Athlete Mode → Membership → Advocate. Shows how a
one-time product purchase compounds into recurring revenue and advocacy.

## The Plan

### Slide 14 — What Momentum Looks Like
The interim launch milestones that lead to the proof: Day-30 retention, OS
activation, weekly engagement, ritual adoption. These are the checkpoints to be hit
*before* January 2027 — momentum, not just a destination.

### Slide 15 — The Compounding System
Why the whole is greater than the parts: every layer compounds the last. Product
creates entry, ritual creates behavior, the OS creates retention, membership creates
recurring revenue, the ecosystem creates scale.

### Slide 16 — The Moat
Why this is defensible: the more you use AForce, the harder it is to leave. Built
on accumulated performance data, streak psychology, and identity formation — switching
costs that grow over time.

### Slide 17 — The Proof Engine
The go-to-market wedge. Miami and Brickell provide a dense, concentrated population
of high-performance consumers — the ideal place to generate proof quickly and
visibly.

### Slide 18 — Road To America's Real Deal
The sequenced path to the national stage: soft launch → proof of concept →
community scale → national television. Shows the plan is staged and deliberate.

### Slide 19 — What Success Looks Like By January 2027
The Phase 1 target dashboard: 1,000 OS users, 600 DAU, NPS 60+, 60% 60-day
retention, 20% membership conversion, 250 Brickell attendees, 21-day average streak.
Concrete, measurable goals tied to the deadline.

### Slide 20 — The Economics
The unit economics: CAC, LTV, subscription conversion, repeat purchase, gross
margin, and revenue forecast (illustrative assumptions). Translates the behavioral
story into a financial one.

### Slide 21 — Why Invest Today
The closing argument before the ask: behavioral readiness is an open category;
national validation comes through America's Real Deal; membership economics create
recurring revenue; and the GTM is proof-driven, not hope-driven.

### Slide 22 — Projected Investor Returns
The upside, made concrete. Illustrative returns on a 0.125%–0.50% stake at this
round: $25K, $50K, and $100K checks projected to roughly **18× by Year 3** and
**50× by Year 5** — the same multiple holds at every check size. Forward-looking
projections, not a guarantee.

### Slide 23 — Projected Investor Returns · BodyArmor Comparison
What a similar exit would return, anchored to a real category outcome. BodyArmor was
founded in 2011 and acquired by Coca-Cola for **$5.6B** in 2021. At a comparable
exit, a 0.125%–0.50% stake returns roughly **$7M, $14M, and $28M**. An illustrative
hypothetical — comparable outcomes don't predict AForce results.

### Slide 24 — Why AForce Is a Prime Acquisition Target
Strategic acquirers buy category-owning, behavior-driving brands. The comparable
set: **Prime** ($250M+ first-year sales), **BodyArmor** ($5.6B to Coca-Cola),
**Liquid I.V.** ($500M to Unilever), and **Oatly** ($10B+ IPO valuation).
Coca-Cola, Unilever, PepsiCo — AForce is built to be next.
